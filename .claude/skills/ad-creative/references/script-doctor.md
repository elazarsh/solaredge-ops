# Script doctor: mining someone else's scripts

Users often bring scripts they don't like but that contain real facts. Don't polish the old
script. **Harvest it, then rebuild.**

## 1. Harvest facts → `facts.md`
For every line extract only verifiable facts (numbers, benefits, rules, dates, places, people).
Table: id · fact · what it means in plain words · which perception axis it proves · what to verify.
Mark the whole file **unverified** until the client confirms. An unverified number never goes on screen.

## 2. Harvest insights
Look for lines that express a real pain or truth even if badly executed
(e.g. "the bonus that disappears from the payslip"). Keep the truth, drop the phrasing.

## 3. Flag risks
- Comparative/superlative claims ("the best in the region") → drop unless sourced.
- Gendered recruitment copy ("בואי לעבוד") → needs both genders or the inclusive note.
- Offer stated in the first 3 s → violates the twist rule.
- Emoji bullet lists of benefits, "leave your details!" endings → info dump, not persuasion.
- Length > 30 s with one person pointing at text → retention risk.
- Props and gestures that only work with sound or explanation.

## 4. Keep what's good in craft
Useful devices to reuse even from weak scripts: a physical metaphor (a block tower as a salary
ladder), SFX on text hits (whoosh/pop), a recurring prop, a real location, a ritual (Friday challah).
Note them in `facts.md` → "craft worth keeping".

## 5. Rebuild
Map each fact to the concept where it becomes a **reveal**, not a bullet. One ad = 1–3 facts max.
A fact that is surprising on its own (e.g. "your philosophy degree raises your hourly pay") can
become the premise of a whole twist episode.

## 6. Report back briefly
What was taken (facts), what was dropped and why (risks), which episodes the facts now power,
and the exact list of numbers the client must confirm.
