# The twist: smart ads that don't push the message

House rule: **every concept must be indirect.** The ad earns attention with a story, a game or a
surprise, and the message lands as the *payoff*, not the premise. The viewer should feel clever
for getting it — not sold to.

## Anatomy of a twist ad

1. **Setup (0–3 s)** — a familiar, intriguing situation that is *not obviously an ad*.
2. **Misdirection** — we let the viewer assume something (genre, meaning, who's talking).
3. **Reveal / turn** — one moment flips the meaning. Often a single cut, word or sound.
4. **Link** — the flip *is* the product truth (the SMP). The brand appears here, not before.
5. **Button** — a short beat after the reveal (a smile, a callback line) + clear CTA.

If you delete the reveal and the ad still makes sense, there's no twist.
If you delete the brand and the reveal no longer makes sense, the twist is *ownable* — good.

## 15 twist techniques

| # | Technique | How | Example (daycare recruitment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Genre hijack** | Start as another genre (thriller trailer, nature doc, cooking show, news) | Nature-doc narrator: "בבוקר, הלהקה מתאספת…" → reveal: toddlers + staff, "תצטרפי ללהקה" |
| 2 | **Misread situation** | Frame shows something that means X; widen/cut shows Y | "20 אנשים חיכו לה בבוקר" (celebrity vibe) → 20 toddlers waving |
| 3 | **Job-title reframe** | Describe the job with exaggerated/other-profession words | "דרושה: מנהלת אירועים, שפית, מדענית, בדרנית" → "או בקיצור: מטפלת" |
| 4 | **Role reversal** | The expected giver is the receiver | Kids "interview" the candidate: "את יודעת לעשות פרצוף של אריה?" |
| 5 | **Literal idiom** (strong in Hebrew) | Visualize a Hebrew expression literally | "לב גדול" / "ידיים מלאות עבודה" / "עם הראש בעננים" shown literally → twist to meaning |
| 6 | **Understatement** | Treat something big as tiny, deadpan | "עבודה רגילה. רק שמחבקים אותך 40 פעם ביום." |
| 7 | **Wrong expectation of the ending** | Build to the obvious punchline, then land a different one | Countdown "3 סיבות לא לעבוד אצלנו" → each reason is a perk in disguise |
| 8 | **Unexpected narrator** | Story told by an object, a kid, a building, a future self | The mountain (הר ברכה) narrates who arrives each morning |
| 9 | **Fake UI / format break** | Chat, notification, job app, loading bar that behaves unexpectedly | A WhatsApp chat with "אמא" that turns out to be 12 kids calling "גננת!" |
| 10 | **Before/after swap** | The "after" is surprising or the order is reversed | "Before": boring office cubicle. "After": finger paint on the same hands |
| 11 | **Hidden in plain sight** | A detail in the first frame only makes sense at the end; invites re-watch | Early sign "יום ראשון שלך" in the background, noticed at the end |
| 12 | **Constraint game** | Tell the story under a rule (one word per shot, only emojis, only sounds) | Whole ad in toddler words: "עוד!", "שוב!", "את!" |
| 13 | **Quiz / choose** | Viewer is asked to guess; the answer surprises | "מה המשכורת הכי שווה שקיבלת?" → a crayon drawing "לגננת שלי" |
| 14 | **Scale shift** | Tiny thing framed epic or epic thing framed tiny | Epic movie trailer music for "the first time a child tied their shoe" |
| 15 | **Callback loop** | Last line echoes the first with new meaning | Opens: "יש לך ניסיון?" Ends: "לא צריך ניסיון. צריך לב." |

## Writing process (do this for each concept)

1. Write the SMP in one line. Write the **most obvious ad** for it in one line — that's the thing to avoid.
2. List 10 associations with the product/audience (objects, sounds, idioms, rituals, pains).
3. Collide 2–3 twist techniques with those associations → 5 rough premises, one line each.
4. Pick the premise that passes all five tests below; develop it into setup → misdirect → reveal → link → button.
5. Write 3 hooks for it (from `hooks-he.md`) that set up the misdirection without giving it away.

## The five tests

| Test | Question | Fail → |
|---|---|---|
| **Surprise** | Would a viewer guess the ending from the first 3 s? | Hide more / change the misdirect |
| **Clarity** | After the reveal, can a 12-year-old say what's offered in one sentence? | Make the link line explicit |
| **Truth** | Does the twist dramatize a real product truth (not a random gag)? | Reconnect to SMP |
| **Ownable** | With a competitor's logo, does it still work equally well? | Add specific place/people/ritual |
| **Kind** | Does the humor punch down at anyone (age, gender, community)? | Rewrite |

## Clever ≠ confusing
- Brand/offer must be unmistakable by the last 3 s: product name, what it is, one action.
- One twist per ad. Twist on twist loses people.
- The reveal needs a clear **visual** moment (sound-off viewers!) — a cut, a zoom-out, a text flip.
- Match twist intensity to age (`audiences.md`): absurd/meta for teens, situational for adults,
  warm/wise surprise for seniors, silly/magic for kids.
- For recruitment/job ads the twist must still state the role, place and how to apply at the end.
