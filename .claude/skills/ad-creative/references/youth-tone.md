# Speaking to young Israelis (18–30)

What works:
- **Authentic over polished.** Content that looks like a real person made it (UGC, phone
  framing, handwritten-style captions) beats corporate gloss. Real faces, real places, real numbers.
- **Talk like a friend, not like HR.** Short sentences, second person, everyday Hebrew.
  "בואי להכיר" > "הנך מוזמנת לפנות". Avoid מליצות and bureaucratic words (הנ"ל, בהתאם, יש לפנות).
- **Light slang, carefully.** One touch ("וואלה", "סבבה", "על הכיפאק") can warm it; forced slang
  reads as a brand trying too hard and dates fast. When unsure, skip slang and use rhythm and humor instead.
- **Values and belonging.** Meaning, team, community, flexibility, being close to home — shown, not claimed.
- **Humor and self-awareness** work; condescension and "מתיילד" tone don't.
- **Speed.** Cuts every 1.5–2.5 s, text that pops on the beat, music with energy.
- **Emoji** in on-screen text: 0–2 per ad, as punctuation, never replacing words.

Sector notes (adapt, don't stereotype): religious/national-religious communities — modest
visuals and wording, no mixed-dancing/party imagery, respect for Shabbat in timing
("לא עובדים בשבת" can be a benefit). Check with the user when unsure.

Copy polish checklist:
- Read every line aloud. If it's hard to say, it's hard to read.
- Replace adjectives with facts ("שכר הוגן" → "שכר מעל המינימום + ותק", only if true).
- Cut 30% of the words from the first draft.
- Every line: could a 22-year-old say it to a friend on WhatsApp? If not, rewrite.
