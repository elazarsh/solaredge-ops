# AI adoption playbook

Adoption = changing work processes, tools/skills and culture - not buying a tool. (A cited 2025 study claims ~95% of GenAI pilots show no measurable profit impact, mostly generic tools not wired into workflows - treat as indicative.)

## Small-business shortcut
One repetitive time-consuming task -> one tool -> one week of practice -> then the next structurally similar process (WhatsApp replies -> email replies). First wins: WhatsApp customer support, social/email drafting, quotes, meeting summaries, document summaries. Track minutes/hours per week before vs after; expect first time saving in 1-2 weeks. Start with free tiers; no technical team needed. Mistakes: starting with the most complicated process; not tracking time saved.

## Maturity model
1. Ad hoc (personal accounts, no visibility) 2. Decentralised experiments (some licences, nothing measured) 3. Managed (policy, approved tools, department owners, 2-3 piloted processes with metrics) 4. AI-first (embedded in core processes, agents, role-based training, quarterly ROI to leadership).
Self-check: read policy? paid approved tool? named owner? one process measured before/after? regular reporting? (0-1 = L1, 2-3 = L2, 4 = L3, 5 + agents in core = L4).

## Six steps
1. 2-3 measurable **business** goals. 2. Map recurring tasks; classify AI-only / human-in-loop / human-only; pick 3-5 use cases by value and ease (back-office often best ROI). 3. Data, tools, security: what may enter tools, business-tier licences, permissions before connecting. 4. **Pilot** 4-6 weeks, 5-15 people, one process, success criteria in advance, sandbox, scalable. 5. Role-based training + champions. 6. Scale via templates/saved prompts/automations department by department; quarterly report.

## 90-day plan
D1-14 owner + steering group, anonymous usage survey (incl. personal tools), goals. D15-30 policy, approved tools, process mapping, 3 use cases. D31-45 basic training, **baseline measurement**, champions. D46-75 pilot 2-3 teams, 30-min weekly status, document working prompts. D76-90 compare to baseline, expand/stop each use case, leadership readout, next quarter.

## Governance levels & failure points
Organisation (strategy, budget, policy; gap between plan and floor) / Department (owner, pilots, measurement; automating unmapped processes, counting logins as success) / Individual (champions, community; tool overload, training without outcomes).

## Strategies
Gradual (one tool, expand), deep single stack, bottom-up "jungle" (security risk), flood (no focus). Choose by leadership involvement, budget, top-down vs bottom-up pressure.

## Shadow AI
Blanket bans fail. Provide a good approved tool, a short clear policy naming prohibited data, an anonymous survey in week 1.

## Usage-policy essentials (template, not legal advice)
Business-tier tools only for organisational data; prohibited inputs (ID numbers, financial/medical data, employee personal data, identifiable customer data unless approved, credentials/API keys, NDA material); human review before output reaches customers/regulators/public (responsibility stays with the employee); label AI content where required; report incidents within ~24 h; review every ~6 months or when adding a tool.

## Change management
Process change, cognitive change (explain why), emotional change (address job fears directly), discourse change (share small wins, leaders use tools visibly). Early small success + champions + 1:1 coaching.

## Metrics
Adoption (weekly active users in the approved tool), Efficiency (task time before/after on ~20 tasks), Quality (share accepted without major revision, manager/customer scores), Financial (hours saved x hourly cost - licences - training, quarterly). No baseline = no ROI. Don't use token consumption as a success metric.

## Israel note
Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 (in force 14 Aug 2025) expands enforcement: business-tier tools with data-processing agreements, update data maps to include AI tools, add prohibited-input rules to the security procedure. Verify with a professional.

## Pitfalls
Tool purchase = adoption; pilot without owner/process/metric; relying on one star employee; no data/security stage; one massive project vs small wins; training not tied to work outcomes.
